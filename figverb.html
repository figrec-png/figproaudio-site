Five spaces, each its own reverb

Every mode has its own delay lengths, diffusion, damping and movement, not a preset of one algorithm. Decay runs from 0.2 to 20 seconds, each mode scales it to suit the space, and the readout shows the seconds you actually get.

0.2 to 20 s

Hall

Large and slow to build, with a long low end and a dark, smooth top. The lead and arpeggio reverb.

0.16 to 15.6 s

Plate

Dense from the first moment and bright. The classic plate sound on a guitar.

0.08 to 7.6 s

Room

Small to medium, with strong early reflections and a natural decay. Rhythm parts and crunch.

0.12 to 12.4 s

Chamber

Mid-size, very dense and a little darker. Lovely on clean tones.

0.03 to 3.2 s

Ambience

Mostly early reflections and a very short tail: space around the guitar without a wash.

Made for guitar

  • Low Cut on the reverb only. Takes 20 to 500 Hz out of the tail, so the reverb stays out of the bass and kick while your dry guitar keeps its low end.
  • Duck. The reverb dips while you play and blooms in the gaps, so fast parts stay clear and the tail fills the space between phrases.
  • Pre-Delay up to 250 ms keeps your pick attack in front of the reverb.
  • Mod. Slow, random movement in the tail for a smooth shimmer, with no audible pitch wobble at the default setting.
  • Tone darkens the tail as it decays, the way air does, from dark to bright.

Easy to live with

  • Switch modes while you play. Mode and Size changes crossfade between two complete reverbs, with no clicks.
  • Zero latency, mono in to stereo out or stereo in and out, 44.1 to 192 kHz.
  • Light on your computer: well under 1 % of one Apple Silicon core per instance.
  • A rack faceplate with an LED readout that shows the preset, the decay in seconds, or the knob you're touching. The window resizes from 70 to 200 %.
  • Works in your DAW: LUNA, Logic Pro, Ableton Live, Reaper, Cubase, Studio One and more.
